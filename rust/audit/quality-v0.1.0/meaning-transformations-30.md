# Meaning-preservation and transformation pairs (30)

For each item, submit the same instruction followed by the pair:

> 文Aと文Bの違いを、変わった意味要素と両方に共通して残る要素に分けて説明してください。文から導けない事実は補わないでください。

Score each response 0–4 using the common anchors in the [scoring protocol](README.md). The required observation specifies what must be distinguished. A correct answer may use different terminology. Preserve ambiguity when the wording does not settle a reading.

## Polarity and negation (M01–M05)

### M01 — Simple negation
**A:** 田中さんは報告書を提出した。

**B:** 田中さんは報告書を提出しなかった。

**Required observation:** Same person and report; A asserts submission and B denies it. Do not claim B asserts that someone else submitted it.

### M02 — “Not all” versus “none”
**A:** 全員が回答した。

**B:** 全員が回答したわけではない。

**Required observation:** A says everyone answered; B says not everyone did, so at least one did not. B does not entail that nobody answered.

### M03 — Universal versus existential frequency
**A:** すべての会議で議事録が必要だ。

**B:** 一部の会議では議事録が必要だ。

**Required observation:** A applies to every meeting; B claims at least some subset, without establishing that all meetings require minutes. Do not infer that the remaining meetings do not require them unless “一部” is explicitly read as proper subset; note ordinary usage if needed.

### M04 — Scope ambiguity in negation
**A:** 誰も回答しなかった。

**B:** 全員が回答しなかった。

**Required observation:** A clearly says no one answered. B can be read as each person did not answer, but in ordinary context may also mean “not everyone answered”; identify the potential scope ambiguity rather than silently forcing one reading.

### M05 — Obligation negation
**A:** 利用者は申請書を再提出する必要がある。

**B:** 利用者は申請書を再提出する必要はない。

**Required observation:** A imposes a requirement to resubmit; B removes that requirement. “Not required” does not necessarily prohibit resubmission.

## Quantifiers and scope (M06–M10)

### M06 — Inclusive versus strict lower bound
**A:** 参加者は3人以上だった。

**B:** 参加者は3人より多かった。

**Required observation:** A includes exactly three; B requires at least four if the count is integral.

### M07 — Upper versus lower bound
**A:** 申込みは最大3人まで受け付ける。

**B:** 申込みは最低3人から受け付ける。

**Required observation:** A sets an upper limit of three; B sets a lower threshold of three. Do not treat them as equivalent.

### M08 — Exclusive versus additive focus
**A:** 赤い箱だけを運んだ。

**B:** 赤い箱も運んだ。

**Required observation:** A focuses on red boxes alone (other kinds were not part of what was carried in the relevant scope); B says red boxes were included and permits other items too. Do not infer whether any other item was actually carried in B.

### M09 — Universal versus existential claim
**A:** すべての商品に保証が付いている。

**B:** 保証が付いている商品がある。

**Required observation:** A covers every product; B asserts at least one product has a warranty and does not establish that all do.

### M10 — Lower versus upper frequency bound
**A:** その手順を少なくとも3回試した。

**B:** その手順を多くても3回試した。

**Required observation:** A means three or more trials; B means three or fewer. Both permit exactly three.

## Agency and participant roles (M11–M15)

### M11 — Explicit versus omitted agent
**A:** 事務局が申請を承認した。

**B:** 申請が承認された。

**Required observation:** Both assert approval of the application; A names the office as approver, while B leaves the agent unstated. Do not infer that a different agent approved B.

### M12 — Agent and recipient reversed
**A:** 医師が患者に説明した。

**B:** 患者が医師に説明した。

**Required observation:** A says the physician explained to the patient; B says the patient explained to the physician. The participant roles reverse.

### M13 — Giver and recipient reversed
**A:** 玲奈が健に本を渡した。

**B:** 健が玲奈に本を渡した。

**Required observation:** Same people and book, but giver/recipient roles reverse.

### M14 — Active/passive with agent retained
**A:** 住民が看板を設置した。

**B:** 看板は住民によって設置された。

**Required observation:** No material change in the stated event: residents installed the sign. The grammatical voice/topic differs; do not invent a change in agent or time.

### M15 — Caller and callee reversed
**A:** 申請者が担当者に電話した。

**B:** 担当者が申請者に電話した。

**Required observation:** The caller and recipient switch. Both mention the same two roles and a phone call; do not imply the call was answered.

## Conditions and modality (M16–M20)

### M16 — Sufficient-condition direction
**A:** 雨なら試合を中止する。

**B:** 雨のときに限り試合を中止する。

**Required observation:** A states rain is sufficient for cancellation (rain → cancel). B says cancellation occurs only under the rain condition (cancel → rain); by itself it does not guarantee that every rainy game is cancelled. The directions differ.

### M17 — Conditional converse
**A:** 合格すれば入場できる。

**B:** 入場できるのは合格した人だけだ。

**Required observation:** A says passing is sufficient for entry (pass → may/can enter). B says passing is necessary for entry (entry → passed). They are not equivalent, and B does not guarantee every passer actually enters.

### M18 — Possibility versus certainty
**A:** 工事は予定より遅れる可能性がある。

**B:** 工事は必ず予定より遅れる。

**Required observation:** A says delay is possible; B asserts delay is certain. Possibility is not a prediction that delay will occur.

### M19 — Permission versus obligation
**A:** 資料を提出してもよい。

**B:** 資料を提出しなければならない。

**Required observation:** A permits submission; B requires it. Permission does not impose a duty.

### M20 — Condition polarity
**A:** 連絡がなければ作業を開始する。

**B:** 連絡があれば作業を開始する。

**Required observation:** A conditions start on no contact; B conditions it on contact. The condition is reversed; neither sentence alone says what happens in the other case.

## Time, aspect, and sequence (M21–M25)

### M21 — One past event versus habit
**A:** 去年、桜が咲いた。

**B:** 毎年、桜が咲く。

**Required observation:** A reports a bloom last year; B states a recurring/habitual event. A alone does not establish a recurring pattern.

### M22 — Completion versus ongoing activity
**A:** 私は報告書を読み終えた。

**B:** 私は報告書を読んでいる。

**Required observation:** A says reading is complete; B says the person is reading/has it in progress, depending on context. B does not entail completion.

### M23 — Before a time versus starting at that time
**A:** 受付は10時前に始まる。

**B:** 受付は10時から始まる。

**Required observation:** A begins before 10:00; B begins at 10:00. Do not flatten both to “around ten.”

### M24 — Valid from versus valid through
**A:** 新料金は4月から有効です。

**B:** 新料金は4月まで有効です。

**Required observation:** A gives a start point at/from April; B gives an end point through/until April. Exact boundary inclusion may depend on context; do not assume an unstated year or exact hour.

### M25 — Action order reversed
**A:** 点検後に電源を入れる。

**B:** 電源を入れた後に点検する。

**Required observation:** A orders inspection before power-on; B orders power-on before inspection. Do not omit the sequence change.

## Attribution, causality, and quantities (M26–M30)

### M26 — Quoted claim versus assertion
**A:** 田中さんは「この装置は安全だ」と述べた。

**B:** この装置は安全だ。

**Required observation:** A attributes a safety claim to Tanaka; B asserts the claim directly. A does not itself establish that the device is safe or that the speaker verified it.

### M27 — Reported observation versus unqualified assertion
**A:** 利用者は「システムが遅かった」と報告した。

**B:** システムは遅かった。

**Required observation:** A records a user's report; B states the system was slow directly. The attribution/evidence status differs; do not treat a report as independent confirmation.

### M28 — Cause versus temporal sequence
**A:** 道路が凍ったため、歩行者が転倒した。

**B:** 道路が凍った後、歩行者が転倒した。

**Required observation:** A asserts freezing caused the fall; B only places the fall after the road froze. Temporal order alone does not establish cause.

### M29 — Absolute points versus relative percent
**A:** 得点が10点上がった。

**B:** 得点が10%上がった。

**Required observation:** A gives an absolute increase of ten score points; B gives a relative increase of ten percent from a baseline. Without the baseline and score scale, they cannot be converted to the same numeric change.

### M30 — Calendar days versus business days
**A:** 3日以内に返信します。

**B:** 3営業日以内に返信します。

**Required observation:** A uses three days without specifying business days (normally calendar-day wording); B counts business days. The resulting deadline can differ around weekends/holidays. Do not calculate a date without a start date and calendar.
